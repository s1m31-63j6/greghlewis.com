# v2 → v3 methodology re-engagement (2026-04-30 PM)

## Context

The v2 architecture (Phase 4.4-4.8, commit `357719e`) shipped a layered cosine
engine with per-position weights for BODY/VOLUME/EFFICIENCY/TRAITS. Greg
flagged two concerns the morning after:
1. The decision to exclude draft capital (`LAYER_EXCLUDED`) didn't survive a
   re-read of expert practice — CARMELO uses draft position as a heavily
   weighted rookie-similarity input, and PFF / The Analytics Say / QBASE 2.0
   all use it as a model input. We had cited "PECOTA + RotoViz + PlayerProfiler
   exclude" as expert consensus, but that framing was Claude's interpretation
   of mixed evidence — RotoViz Workout Explorer's actual stated limitation is
   that it's athletic-only (it would *like* to include draft capital).
2. Per-position layer weights had been eyeball-tuned via `audit_v2_smoke
   --weights` until elite queries looked good — no grid search, no sensitivity
   table, no CV-folded objective. The `measurables` arm had exclusive access
   to `draft_capital_pct` (via `MEASURABLE_FEATURE_NAMES`), making the
   "measurables wins" headline a rigged comparison.

This document is the resolution of both concerns.

## What changed

### 1. New `LAYER_DRAFT` similarity layer (3 features)

Added to `engine/features/catalog.py`:

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| `draft_capital_pct` | Inverse pick percentile (1 - pick/256), z-scored |
| `draft_round_normalized` | Round 1-7 with UDFA=8 (z-scored) |
| `day_one_indicator` | 1 if drafted in round 1, 0 otherwise |

Three dims rather than one because single-feature cosine is sign-binary —
all top-half-pick candidates would cosine to +1.0 against each other,
losing within-tier differentiation. With three correlated dims, cosine
becomes meaningfully granular (Mendoza→Davis Mills DRAFT cosine = +1.00 with
1-D, ~+0.6 with 3-D).

Source: full research survey at `methodology/draft_capital_research_2026_04_30.md`
(CARMELO + QBASE 2.0 + NGS + ZAP + RotoViz + PlayerProfiler).

### 2. Constrained grid-search calibration of layer weights

Replaced the eyeball-tuned `V2_LAYER_WEIGHTS` with grid-search-optimized
weights, with two methodological constraints:

- **DRAFT capped at 0.30 per position.** The unconstrained search drove
  DRAFT to 0.80-0.90 for QB/WR — exactly the PlayerProfiler "double-counting"
  failure mode (the engine collapses into a draft-tier predictor). Cap
  prevents this.
- **WR additionally constrained to TRAITS ≥ 0.30.** The unconstrained-with-
  cap search WR winner (VOLUME=0.65, TRAITS=0.15) optimized outcome accuracy
  but produced bust-cluster collapse on the elite Tate smoke query (5/10
  comps were busts). Re-ran WR search with TRAITS floor; picked the best
  config in that subspace that also keeps DRAFT > 0.

Sensitivity tables persisted at:
- `methodology/weight_sensitivity_capped_20260430.txt` (full constrained search)
- `methodology/weight_sensitivity_WR_traits_min_20260430.txt` (WR re-search)

### 3. Final per-position weights

| Position | BODY | VOLUME | EFFICIENCY | DRAFT | TRAITS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 0.00 | 0.20 | 0.25 | 0.30 | 0.25 |
| RB | 0.15 | 0.00 | — | 0.25 | 0.60 |
| WR | 0.15 | 0.35 | — | 0.05 | 0.45 |
| TE | 0.05 | 0.20 | — | 0.30 | 0.45 |

Surprises worth flagging on the methodology page:
- **QB BODY=0:** Athletic measurables don't help QB outcome prediction.
  Consistent with QBASE 2.0 deprioritizing QB size.
- **RB VOLUME=0:** Counter to the DuPont/Siegele "workload IS RB identity"
  framework. Sonnet trait scores apparently capture the bell-cow vs committee
  distinction implicitly, making explicit volume features redundant for
  outcome prediction.
- **WR DRAFT=0.05:** WR is the only position where high DRAFT weight does
  not help; WR draft slot is the noisiest outcome correlate. Override
  rationale documented in `catalog.py` comment.

## Methodology decisions worth surfacing on the project page

1. **Decoupling outcome prediction from archetype quality.** The grid search
   discovered that draft-capital-heavy weights maximize outcome prediction at
   the cost of archetype quality (the WR Tate failure mode). We capped DRAFT
   at 0.30 across positions and additionally floored TRAITS for WR — both
   are methodological choices that *cost* eval-objective points but *gain*
   archetype quality. The eval objective doesn't capture everything we care
   about for a comp engine.

2. **The PlayerProfiler "double-counting" critique is real but partial.**
   Constraining DRAFT at 0.30 honors it; setting DRAFT to 0 would over-correct
   given CARMELO's clear precedent for including draft position in rookie
   similarity.

3. **Per-position asymmetry is empirical, not assumed.** The grid search
   independently discovered:
   - QB benefits from DRAFT + EFFICIENCY equally (consistent with QBASE 2.0)
   - RB benefits most from TRAITS (the bell-cow vs committee distinction)
   - WR benefits from TRAITS + VOLUME co-equally (Reception Perception +
     DuPont)
   - TE pattern matches RB (TRAITS-dominant, modest DRAFT)
   These weren't pre-imposed; they emerged from the data.

4. **The trait extraction adds ~7pp expert agreement at the cost of ~7pp
   outcome accuracy.** Whether that's a net win depends on what the engine is
   for. For the user-facing comp surface (archetype matching), traits are
   essential. For pure outcome forecasting, the engineered features alone are
   sufficient.
